Ask three salons about frizz and you may get three different answers: keratin, cysteine, nanoplasty. All three smooth hair, they cost different amounts, and they don't do the same thing. This guide helps you pick.

## The quick answer

- **Choose nanoplasty** if you want strong smoothing or straightening, longer wear, and no formaldehyde, and you're fine paying more.
- **Choose keratin** if you want a proven, lower-cost frizz fix and don't mind a short no-wash period, or you're happy with a formaldehyde-free keratin formula.
- **Choose cysteine** if you want the gentlest option with the most natural finish, and lighter results are fine.

## Side-by-side

| | Nanoplasty | Keratin | Cysteine |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Main result** | Deep smoothing + straightening | Frizz control, smoother texture | Gentle smoothing, keeps movement |
| **Strength** | Strong | Medium–strong | Light–medium |
| **Typically lasts** | 3–6 months | 2–5 months | 6 weeks–3 months |
| **Formaldehyde** | Marketed as free (verify product) | Classic formulas release it; free versions exist | Free |
| **Wash wait** | Often same or next day | Often 2–3 days for classic formulas | Usually none or short |
| **Typical price range** | Highest | Mid | Low–mid |

Exact durations and prices vary by product, stylist, and hair. Use these as typical ranges, not promises.

## Five questions to decide

### 1. How much change do you want?

If you want hair that looks blown out after air-drying, that's nanoplasty or a strong keratin. If you like your texture and just want less frizz, cysteine or a light keratin is plenty.

### 2. How do you feel about formaldehyde?

Classic keratin and Brazilian-style treatments release formaldehyde when heated. That's the main reason many people look at nanoplasty and cysteine. Be aware that "formaldehyde-free" on a label isn't the same as "no concerns": some formaldehyde-free straighteners rely on glyoxylic acid, which has its own open safety questions. See [is nanoplasty really formaldehyde-free?](/blog/is-nanoplasty-formaldehyde-free/) and our [keratin safety guide](/is-keratin-safe/).

### 3. How long do you want it to last?

If you only want to book twice a year, nanoplasty's longer wear helps offset the higher price. If you're testing smoothing for the first time, a shorter-lasting option is lower-risk.

### 4. What's your hair's condition?

Heavily bleached or fragile hair needs a careful stylist no matter what. Some will steer you toward cysteine or a bond-repair service first and smoothing later. Healthy hair, colored or not, usually handles any of the three.

### 5. What's your budget, really?

Compare the cost per month, not the price per visit. A nanoplasty that lasts five months can cost less per month than a cheaper treatment you redo every eight weeks. Our [Chicago nanoplasty cost guide](/blog/nanoplasty-cost-chicago/) breaks this down.

## Common scenarios

- **"My wavy hair frizzes every summer and I want to air-dry."** Nanoplasty.
- **"I have thick, coarse hair and want maximum smoothness on a budget."** Traditional keratin, or an [express keratin](/) to test it first.
- **"I'm nervous about chemicals and want something subtle."** Cysteine. Read [what a cysteine treatment is](/cysteine-treatment/).
- **"I have tight curls and want them softer, not straight."** Talk to a stylist about a light nanoplasty or cysteine. See [nanoplasty for curly hair](/blog/nanoplasty-for-curly-hair/).
- **"My hair is damaged and I mainly want it to feel healthier."** Consider a repair treatment first. [Hair botox vs. keratin](/hair-botox-vs-keratin/) explains the difference.
