"Formaldehyde-free" is the main reason many people choose nanoplasty over a classic keratin or Brazilian-style treatment. It's a reasonable reason, but the label alone doesn't tell you everything. Here's how to check for yourself.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. If you're pregnant, have respiratory or kidney conditions, or have concerns about specific ingredients, talk to your doctor before any chemical hair treatment.*

## Why formaldehyde is the concern

Many traditional smoothing treatments use formaldehyde or ingredients that release it when heated by a flat iron. Formaldehyde is a known irritant and is classified as a human carcinogen, which is why salon ventilation matters and why many clients and stylists look for alternatives. Our [keratin safety guide](/is-keratin-safe/) covers this in more depth.

## Where "formaldehyde-free" gets complicated

### Hidden names

Formaldehyde doesn't always appear by that name. On an ingredient list, watch for:

- **Formaldehyde** / **formalin**
- **Methylene glycol** (formaldehyde dissolved in water)
- **Methanal**
- **Oxomethane**

A product sold as formaldehyde-free shouldn't list any of these.

### Glyoxylic acid

Many formaldehyde-free straighteners, nanoplasty included, rely on **glyoxylic acid** or related compounds to do the smoothing. It isn't formaldehyde, but researchers and regulators are still looking at its safety, including reports of rare kidney problems associated with some glyoxylic-acid straightening products. The research is evolving, and rules differ by country.

That doesn't automatically make a product unsafe. It does mean "formaldehyde-free" shouldn't be read as "no questions to ask."

### Marketing language

Terms like "organic," "natural," and "nano" aren't regulated safety claims for hair straighteners. Ask for the actual ingredient list.

## Your five-step check

1. **Get the product name** from the salon.
2. **Read the ingredient list**, on the product or the brand's website.
3. **Look for the formaldehyde names** above.
4. **Note whether glyoxylic acid is listed**, and decide with your doctor if you have kidney concerns or other health conditions.
5. **Check the salon's ventilation.** Any heated chemical treatment should be done in a well-ventilated space.

## Questions to ask your stylist

- Which product do you use, and can I see the ingredients?
- Is it free of formaldehyde **and** formaldehyde releasers?
- Does it contain glyoxylic acid?
- What's your ventilation like during the flat-iron step?
- Have any clients reacted to it, such as scalp irritation or burning eyes?

A good stylist welcomes these questions. More in our [9 questions to ask before booking](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-nanoplasty/).

## If you want the gentlest option

If ingredient questions are a dealbreaker, consider:

- **[Cysteine treatments](/cysteine-treatment/)**: amino-acid-based, gentler, with lighter results.
- **Repair treatments** (sometimes sold as "hair botox"): conditioning, not straightening. See [hair botox vs. keratin](/hair-botox-vs-keratin/).
- **[Formaldehyde-free keratin](/formaldehyde-free-keratin/)**: check the label just as carefully.

## Bottom line

Nanoplasty is generally marketed as formaldehyde-free, and many products are. But the only way to know is to read the label for your specific product, and to know that its alternative active ingredients have their own open questions. Ask, read, and decide with good information.
