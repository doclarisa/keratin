If your hair behaves on vacation and falls apart the moment you're back in Chicago, it isn't your imagination. Few climates are as hard on hair as ours: sticky, humid summers, then months of cold air outside and dry radiator heat inside, plus water that leaves a film over time.

Here's what each of those does to your hair, and why a smoothing treatment like nanoplasty holds up better than a blowout.

## Summer: humidity frizz

Chicago summers are humid, and July and August are usually the worst. Hair is hygroscopic, meaning it absorbs water from the air. When the cuticle is raised, from coarse texture, coloring, or heat styling, humid air swells some strands more than others, and that uneven swelling is frizz.

That's why a blowout can look perfect at 8 a.m. and puff up by the time you cross the parking lot. Styling products help for a few hours, but they sit on the surface.

**How nanoplasty helps:** it smooths and seals the cuticle from the inside, so less moisture gets in. Instead of fighting frizz every morning, your hair starts the day closer to smooth. For more on humidity, see [smoothing treatments for frizz and humidity](/keratin-for-frizz-humidity/).

## Winter: dry air and static

From roughly November through March, the problem flips. Cold outdoor air holds little moisture, and indoor heat dries it out further. Hair loses moisture, gets brittle at the ends, and turns staticky under hats and scarves.

**How nanoplasty helps:** a sealed cuticle holds onto moisture better, and the smoother surface means less friction and static. It doesn't replace conditioner or a leave-in, but hair is noticeably easier to manage.

## Year-round: what's in the water

Chicago's tap water comes from Lake Michigan and is considered moderately hard, meaning it carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Over time those minerals can build up on the hair and make it feel rough, dull, and harder to style. If you're in an older building, the pipes can add their own deposits.

**What this means for nanoplasty:**

- Your stylist will usually start with a **clarifying wash** to strip buildup so the treatment absorbs evenly. That step matters even more with hard water.
- At home, a **sulfate-free clarifying or chelating shampoo** once or twice a month, if your stylist approves it for your treatment, keeps buildup from dulling the result.
- A **shower filter** is an inexpensive extra that some people find makes a noticeable difference.

## Lake days and pools

Lake Michigan beaches, pool memberships, and summer swim lessons are part of a Chicago summer. Chlorine and salt both shorten the life of any smoothing treatment. Before swimming:

1. Wet your hair with clean tap water first, so it soaks up less pool water.
2. Apply a leave-in conditioner.
3. Rinse right after swimming, and use your sulfate-free shampoo the same day.

## When Chicagoans book nanoplasty

Because nanoplasty usually lasts 3–6 months, many people plan around the seasons:

- **Late May or early June:** smooth for the humid stretch through August.
- **October or November:** ready for holiday photos and the dry-air months.

Booking ahead of humid season also means you're not trying to get an appointment in the middle of July, when frizz is at its worst and calendars fill up.

## Bottom line

Chicago gives your hair two opposite problems, humidity in summer and dryness in winter, plus hard water all year. A smoothing treatment works because it changes how your hair responds to all three, instead of patching it with styling products every morning.
